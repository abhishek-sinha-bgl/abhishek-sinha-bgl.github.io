About

Abhishek Sinha

On technology, organisations, AI and the questions that emerge as they change.

Background

I have spent much of my working life in enterprise technology, working with large organisations as they build, change and sometimes rethink the systems on which they depend. My background includes enterprise architecture, global technology services, transformation and leadership roles at Hewlett-Packard, IBM and Kyndryl. I currently work with Arth Group.

That experience has left me interested in the distance between what technology promises and what happens when people and organisations begin to use it. The most interesting questions are often not settled when a system goes live. They emerge afterwards, as the technology encounters the realities of work, judgment, incentives and institutional habits.

Current interests

AI has made this an especially fascinating time. We are learning to build systems that can reason across information, contribute to decisions and undertake work that until recently seemed firmly within the human domain. Yet it is far from clear how much of their eventual impact we can see today. Even an enterprise that deploys AI successfully may discover new ways of working, new questions about expertise and responsibility, or opportunities that were not part of its original plans. I find that possibility as intriguing as the technology itself.

I am interested in what this means for organisations and the people within them: how human judgment changes when AI becomes a participant in everyday work, how we distinguish a convincing answer from a well-supported one, and what governance and assurance can genuinely establish. Enterprise strategy, regulation, the economics of technology and outsourcing relationships are part of that wider picture, rather than subjects I claim to have neatly resolved.

Some of these interests take a practical form in Epistamate, where I explore research and advisory systems that keep evidence, disagreement and uncertainty visible as conclusions evolve. Others find their way into the essays on this site and Epistamate Insights. I write partly to share what I have learned, and partly to think more carefully about what we have yet to understand.
